Q1 Wellbeing-Newsletter Format Pilot — Retrospective
Author: Priya Shah, People Ops Communications
Period covered: Jan 6 – Mar 28, 2025

1. Headline
The pilot run from Jan to Mar with 1,100 subscribers to the optional internal wellbeing newsletter, comparing a shorter, card-style version against the old long-form version across twelve weekly issues. The card-style issues averaged a 58% open rate against a 41% baseline for the long-form issues from the prior quarter. Click-through also rose by 6 points over the same period.

2. Format performance
The card layout was faster to get through. Median read time for card issues was 1 min 40 s, compared with 4 min 10 s for the long-form layout. Readers of card issues clicked an average of 2.3 links per open, versus 1.1 for long-form. Scroll depth was also better: 71% of card-issue readers reached the last card, while 38% of long-form readers reached the final section.

Clicks were spread unevenly across the recurring sections. Recipes was the most-clicked section in 9 of the 12 issues. The step-challenge recap came second overall and peaked in the weeks after team standings were posted. The desk-stretch tip had the lowest click volume, but it had the highest rate of replies and forwards, which suggests people read it inline and did not need to click through. The long-form layout buried both the step-challenge recap and the desk-stretch tip below the fold, and that alone likely accounts for part of the gap.

3. What worked
Keeping each card to a single topic with one link made the issues easy to scan. It also gave us a much cleaner read on which content people actually wanted. The fixed section order helped as well: readers told us they knew where to look for the recipe each week. The weekly cadence held up, and unsubscribes stayed flat at roughly 0.4% per issue.

4. What didn't
Discoverability of past issues was weak. Several readers wrote in asking for an earlier recipe, and there feedback was mostly positive but pointed out that the archive link was hard to find on the intranet. Right now it sits in the page footer, under two other menus. We also saw mobile rendering issues in two issues, where cards stacked out of order in one email client. The optional reader survey got a 12% response rate, which is lower than we'd like for drawing conclusions about individual sections.

5. Recommendations for Q2
- Keep the card layout as the default for the weekly wellbeing newsletter and retire the long-form template.
- Move the archive link into the newsletter header and add a matching link on the intranet landing page.
- Try a Friday send time for four issues and compare open and click rates against the current Tuesday send.
- We should expand pilot to the monthly events digest next, since two team admins have asked for the same format.
