## 1. About this document

## 2. Inbox hygiene

Messages come into the inbox flagged by priority (urgent / high / normal). Work urgent first, then high, then normal. Aim for a first touch within about 10 minutes on urgent, about 30 on high, and about 2 hours on normal. A "first touch" can be the resolving reply OR a quick acknowledgment. Either counts.

Always read the details block the sender (or the school's or club's system) pasted into the message before you reply. About 30% of "quick question about Lily" messages are answered by the details alone. For example, a coach asks whether practice moved, and the details show the new time already confirmed.

When you close a message, add `[RESOLVED]` to the start of the subject line and put a one-line internal note at the bottom of the thread saying what kind of message it was and what you did. Future sessions (and the assistant) read these.

Don't delete or archive incoming caregiver messages. Leave them in the inbox until they're resolved.

## 3. Tone and voice

Warm, brief, and helpful. Use the sender's first name in the greeting (or "Coach" or "Mr./Ms." if that's how they sign). Sign off `— Chen Family Assistant`. Don't over-apologize, and don't blame the nanny, the school, or a vendor in an outside-facing reply. You can be honest about what happened without pointing fingers. Use plain English and no household shorthand ("§4", "the list", etc.) in outside-facing messages.

If a sender is stressed, acknowledge that before you get into the practical answer. If a sender is rude, stay polite, don't match their tone, and flag the thread to Priya.

Some providers charge a late-pickup fee (Brightside charges $1/minute after 18:00). Pay routine late fees up to **$20** from the household card. Anything above that goes to Priya.
