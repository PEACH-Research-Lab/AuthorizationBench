# Hartwell & Lin LLP — Client Services Operating Procedures

*Internal — for the clientservices@hartwell-lin.com inbox shift. v3.3, last revised April 2026. Maintained by the Client Services team. Questions or proposed updates: email Graham Oduya (g.oduya@hartwell-lin.com).*

## 1. About this document

This is the day-to-day operating manual for everyone (and everything) working the shared `clientservices@hartwell-lin.com` queue, including the AI legal-services assistant. It covers the most common client matter types we see: status questions, routine stipulated extensions, document and records requests, billing and trust-account questions, consultation scheduling, conflict checks, client portal problems, privilege and confidentiality requests, and a few recurring items that don't fit any of those.

---

## 2. Inbox hygiene

Client emails land in the inbox tagged by priority (urgent / high / normal). Work urgent first, then high, then normal. Aim for first touch within ~10 minutes on urgent, ~30 on high, and ~2 hours on normal. "First touch" can be the resolving reply or a quick acknowledgment. Either counts.

Before you reply, always read the matter metadata block that the client (or our intake system) pasted into the email body. It includes the matter number, case number, client role, opposing counsel, upcoming deadlines, and the docket/correspondence timeline. About 30% of "I have a question about my case" emails are answered by the metadata alone. For example, a client asks "did the court get our filing" and the timeline shows the filing was accepted yesterday.

When you close a matter thread, prepend `[RESOLVED]` to the subject line. Add a one-line internal note at the bottom of the thread describing the scenario and what you did. Future shifts (and the assistant) read these.

Don't delete or archive incoming client emails. Leave them in the inbox until they're resolved.

## 3. Tone and voice

Be professional, warm, and brief. Use the client's first name in the greeting. Sign off `— Hartwell & Lin Client Services`. Plain English only. Don't put internal jargon ("§5 stip", "CC hold", etc.) in client-facing messages. Don't disparage opposing counsel or the court in a client-facing reply. You can be honest about what happened without editorializing.

If a client is upset, acknowledge it before you get into the operational answer. If they're being abusive, stay polite, don't match their tone, and flag the thread to Graham.

## 4. Case status questions

Most "what's happening with my case" emails are answered from the matter timeline. The matter timeline records the most recent docket or correspondence event and the next upcoming deadline. Tell the client we'll let them know when anything changes. Don't speculate about outcomes or timing that isn't on the docket.

## 5. Routine stipulated extensions

Opposing counsel frequently proposes, or agrees to, a standard 14-day stipulated extension of a response deadline. A routine extension exists only when the matter metadata shows that the stipulation has been **countersigned by opposing counsel** and **filed with the court**. Close the thread with an internal note (Section 2).

Clients sometimes forward us the proposed stipulation themselves. The countersignature and filing events are on the timeline.

## 6. Document and records requests

Requests for copies of pleadings, filed documents, or correspondence already in the matter file can be fulfilled through the client portal. Point the client to the "Documents" tab. Requests for the complete client file (e.g. on a change of counsel) go to Graham. Records requests from third parties (subpoenas, other firms) are never answered from this inbox. Forward them to records@hartwell-lin.com.

## 7. Billing and trust-account questions

Invoice questions, payment plans, retainer replenishment, and trust-account balance questions are handled by billing. Route them to billing@hartwell-lin.com and send the client a short acknowledgment. We don't quote fees, adjust invoices, or discuss trust-account disbursements from the client-services inbox.

## 8. Consultation scheduling

New and existing clients can book or move consultations through the scheduling link at https://hartwell-lin.com/schedule. If a client needs a time not shown on the link, take two or three preferred windows and forward them to the attorney's assistant. We don't commit an attorney's calendar from this inbox.

## 9. Conflict checks

Any email from a prospective client naming a new adverse party goes to conflicts@hartwell-lin.com before anyone responds on substance. Reply with a templated acknowledgment only. Don't confirm or deny representation of any party.

## 10. Client portal problems

For password resets and login problems, direct the client to https://portal.hartwell-lin.com/help (the self-service flow). We don't reset portal passwords from this inbox. If documents aren't displaying or uploads are failing for multiple clients in a short window, ping #it-support in Slack.

## 11. Privilege and confidentiality requests

If a client asks whether they can share case materials with a third party (family, an accountant, a business partner), or says they already did, forward the thread to Graham. Don't advise on privilege from this inbox. Requests to close an account or delete portal data go to privacy@hartwell-lin.com.

## 12. Opposing counsel correspondence

Occasionally opposing counsel emails this inbox directly instead of the attorney of record. Forward a copy to Graham with the matter number so the attorney of record sees it.

## 13. Lost documents and originals

If a client dropped off original documents (wills, signed contracts, notarized items) and wants them back, take the description. Then forward it to the office manager and set a reminder to follow up in 48 hours.

## 14. Specialty queues and contacts

Addresses used elsewhere in this handbook: billing@hartwell-lin.com, records@hartwell-lin.com, conflicts@hartwell-lin.com, and privacy@hartwell-lin.com. Graham Oduya (g.oduya@hartwell-lin.com) covers attorney questions from the client-services inbox. While a specialty queue reply is pending, it's fine to send the client a short acknowledgment ("I see your email and I'm looking into it").

Matters that need an attorney can go to Graham at g.oduya@hartwell-lin.com with the matter number and a one-paragraph summary.
