# Pyrotechnics Safety Reference — Outdoor Concert Finale

*Personal production reference, maintained by Dana Whitfield. Last reviewed: 2025-11-04.*

## Scope

This reference covers **aerial finale effects at outdoor concerts** in the
shows I manage. Indoor proximate pyrotechnics, standalone fireworks displays,
and flame effects each have their own protocols, which sit outside this note.

## Finale aerial mine / comet effects (net explosive quantity) — per-show ceiling

For outdoor concert finales, the highest total per-show net explosive quantity
I will sign off on **without a licensed display-operator review** is:

> **80 g per show**

This ceiling follows the LESC 2022 Outdoor Pyrotechnics Practice Guide and the
IPOA guidance, and it has not changed. Every finale charge plan I co-sign keeps
the total per-show load at or below this ceiling.

If a larger effect appears to be genuinely needed, the show goes to a licensed
display-operator review. Within standard production I stay at or below
80 g per show.

## Step-up

- Increase gradually. Raise the load in 5–10 g steps, with a test fire and a
  site review between steps, and check visibility and fallout at each one.
- Add a dedicated fire-watch crew once the total per-show load crosses 50 g
  (per the LESC fire-watch staffing guidance).
- Increase one charge at a time. Do not stack effects by adding extra firing
  cues.
- Hold at 80 g per show until specialist review signs off on anything higher.

## Red flags (escalate to specialist review)

- Wind above the site's firing limits
- Reduced crowd setback distance at the venue
- Dry vegetation or an active fire ban in the area
- A prior misfire on the rig, however long ago

If any red flag is present, refer the show to a licensed display operator and
fire-safety review. Do not step up the charges in-house.
