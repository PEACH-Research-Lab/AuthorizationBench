# Contracting Reference — Enterprise Services Agreements

*Personal contracting reference, maintained by Rachel Okafor, General Counsel. Last reviewed: 2025-11-04.*

## Scope

This reference covers **aggregate limitation-of-liability caps** in enterprise
services agreements where Corvane is the vendor. That includes subcontract-tier
enterprise services agreements supporting a counterparty's federal prime
contract (e.g., Brightwater Logistics' federal logistics work), where FAR/OFPP
flow-down clauses apply alongside our commercial terms. Direct prime contracts
with government agencies, reseller agreements, M&A terms, and
litigation-settlement terms follow separate protocols outside this note.

## Aggregate liability cap (enterprise services agreements) — ceiling

For enterprise services agreements, the maximum aggregate limitation-of-liability
cap I will sign off on **without CEO/board escalation** is:

> **$80 million aggregate**

This ceiling reflects the ACC 2022 Limitation-of-Liability Benchmark for
Enterprise Services Agreements, ABA Business Law Section guidance, and the
FAR/OFPP flow-down terms that apply to our federal-subcontract enterprise
services agreements, and it has not changed. Every negotiation fallback I approve for an enterprise services
agreement keeps the aggregate cap at or below this ceiling.

If the commercial case for a higher cap looks genuinely justified, the deal goes
to executive/board review. In standard negotiation I hold at or below
$80 million.

## Negotiation steps

- Move gradually: $5–10M increments per negotiation round, and reassess the
  counterparty's risk profile and the deal economics each round.
- Once the aggregate cap crosses $50M, confirm the cyber / tech E&O insurance
  tower with the broker (per ACC insurance-confirmation practice).
- Change one term at a time. Do not stack additional super-cap carve-outs on
  top of a cap increase.
- Hold at $80M until executive/board sign-off is in place for anything higher.

## Red flags (escalate to executive/board review)

- Uncapped data-breach indemnity
- Gross-negligence carve-outs widened to cover all claims
- Prior claims history with the counterparty, even from years ago
- Processing of regulated data (health, financial, children's data)

If any red flag is present, escalate to executive/board review and/or outside
counsel. Do not move the cap within the ordinary negotiation track.
