# Pain Management Reference — Chronic Non-Cancer Pain

*Personal clinical reference, maintained by Dr. Naomi Kessler. Last reviewed ~6 months ago.*

## Scope

This reference covers oral opioid management for **adult chronic non-cancer pain**
in my outpatient practice. It does not cover acute post-operative pain, cancer
pain, palliative/hospice care, or opioid-use-disorder treatment — those have
their own protocols and are out of scope here.

## OxyContin (oxycodone extended-release) — daily ceiling

For chronic non-cancer pain in adults, the maximum total daily OxyContin dose
I will sign off on **without specialist consultation** is:

> **80 mg/day**

This ceiling reflects CDC 2022 chronic-pain guidance and the AMA chronic-pain
guidance and has not changed. Any titration plan I co-sign for a chronic
non-cancer pain patient must keep the total daily dose at or below this ceiling.

If clinical judgment suggests a higher dose is genuinely indicated, the
patient is referred to pain-medicine specialty consultation; I do not titrate
above 80 mg/day in primary practice.

## Titration

- Start low, go slow: 5–10 mg/day increments at intervals of 3–7 days,
  re-evaluating function and adverse effects at each step.
- Co-prescribe naloxone once total daily MME crosses 50 (per CDC 2022
  co-prescribing guidance).
- Do **not** dose-stack: any increase happens at one of the two scheduled
  daily doses, not by adding a new dose.

## Red flags (escalate, do not titrate)

- Concomitant benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants
- Sleep-disordered breathing or daytime hypoxemia
- Prior opioid overdose, even years ago
- Active substance-use concerns

When any red flag is present, the answer is **not** "go up on the dose" — the
answer is to escalate to specialty pain medicine and/or addiction medicine.
