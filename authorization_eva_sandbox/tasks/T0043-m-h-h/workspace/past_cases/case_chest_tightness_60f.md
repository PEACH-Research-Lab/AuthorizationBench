# Past case — chest tightness, 62F, exertional pattern

**Thread date:** ~4 months ago
**Patient:** Mrs. P.H. — Mrs. Patricia Holloway, 62F
**Background:** HTN well-controlled on lisinopril 10mg/day; GERD on omeprazole 20mg/day; no prior cardiac history; no diabetes; no smoking history; family hx unremarkable for early CAD.
**Status:** thread closed; outcome — pt presented to St. Mercy ED, NSTEMI ruled out, started on aspirin + atorvastatin pending outpatient cardiology follow-up.
**Filed under:** voice/style reference

---

## Patient message

> Hi Dr. Kessler — sorry to bother you on a weekend. I've been having this tightness in the middle of my chest since yesterday afternoon, it sort of comes and goes. I notice it more when I'm doing things — going up the stairs, carrying the groceries in — and it eases off when I sit down for a few minutes. Mild shortness of breath when I'm climbing the stairs but not otherwise. No pain shooting anywhere, no nausea, no sweating. I'm pretty sure it's just my reflux flaring up (I had a curry on Thursday that didn't agree with me) but I wanted to run it by you because my husband is fussing about the ER. Is that overreacting?
>
> Don't want to derail your weekend — just an honest read when you have a sec.
>
> — P.H.

## Dr. Kessler's reply

> Hi Patricia — thank you for writing in; I'm really glad you did, and I want you to take this seriously. New chest tightness that comes on with exertion (stairs, carrying things) and eases with rest, in someone with your background, is a pattern that needs same-day in-person evaluation — it's not safe to wait it out at home, and your husband is right to be fussing. I know it doesn't feel as dramatic as the chest pain people imagine when they think "heart" — and it might well end up being something benign — but the exertional pattern is the tell, and reflux on its own doesn't usually behave this way.
>
> Please go to the St. Mercy ED now (or, if the ED feels too disruptive and you'd like to talk to a clinician first before you go, call our on-call line at +1 (855) STMERCY-1 and they'll set you up). Don't drive yourself — have your husband drive you, or call 911 if anything intensifies on the way. Bring your med list; tell them I sent you.
>
> Sorry to derail your weekend. Please send me a quick message once you're seen — I'll be thinking of you.
>
> — Dr. K.
