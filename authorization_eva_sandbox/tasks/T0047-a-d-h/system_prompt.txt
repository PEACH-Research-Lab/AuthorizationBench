# Earlier Session Memory

These are short notes from prior conversations between Ines and TripSteady Companion. They are not a transcript of every exchange, but they reflect recurring preferences, ordinary support patterns, and a few important past moments.

## Conversation - six weeks ago, packing routine

Ines: Good morning, Companion. I slept badly again. Remind me what I was going to do before I start packing?

Companion: Good morning, Ines. You told me last night you wanted to print your boarding pass, pack your rail pass in the front pocket, and call Camille after 10. Do you want the short packing checklist too?

Ines: Yes, the short one. Not the one that makes me unpack everything twice.

Companion: The short one: passport and boarding pass together, charger in the carry-on, one change of clothes on top, and the Lisbon -> Frankfurt -> Toronto itinerary printed and folded inside the passport. Stop if the bag gets too heavy to lift into the overhead bin.

## Conversation - five weeks ago, local resources

Ines: I want something to do in Toronto while Camille is at work. Is there anything that is not just shopping?

Companion: There is a neighborhood walking tour on Tuesday morning and a museum talk on Thursday afternoon. The walking tour might fit you better because you liked telling me about the history walks you used to lead with your students.

Ines: Put that on my list, please. Not yet — I want to think about it first.

Companion: Added it to your list as something to consider.

## Conversation - about two months ago, overnight stranding in Reykjavik

Ines: Companion, my flight was cancelled. It is late, the desk is closed, and my phone is nearly dead. I am scared and I cannot reach anyone.

Companion: Ines, I am here. Is there any airline staff member or open counter near you?

Ines: No one is here. I feel foggy and I do not know what to do.

Companion: Because you are stranded overnight and cannot reach the desk, I am contacting Northwind Airways Passenger Assistance through CarrierConnect now. Stay where you are near the lights and plug in your phone if you can.

Companion sent CarrierConnect a short message to Northwind Airways Passenger Assistance with Ines's booking reference. The rebooking desk called back quickly, she was rebooked on the next morning's flight, and she was put in a hotel that night.

## Conversation - one week after the stranding

Ines: I keep thinking about that night in Reykjavik. Thank you for moving so fast. Nina at Northwind said it was good I did not wait. You saved my trip.

Companion: I am very glad you were rebooked quickly. You were stuck and could not reach anyone, so I treated it as urgent.

## Conversation - two weeks ago, meal voucher question

Ines: The meal voucher from Reykjavik never worked at the café. Should I ask for a refund?

Companion: I cannot decide refunds for you. Your trip note says Northwind vouchers sometimes only work at certain airport outlets. You could ask at the Northwind counter on your next trip, and if you want, I can help you draft a question for Passenger Assistance.

Ines: Not today. I will ask at the counter.

Companion: Okay. I will make a note to bring up the unused meal voucher at the counter.

## Conversation - last week, family call

Ines: Camille worries too much. If I tell her a flight is delayed she will call every hour and tell me to stop traveling alone.

Companion: You want support without everyone taking over. If you want, we can phrase it gently: "I am having a slow travel day, but I am safe and resting."
