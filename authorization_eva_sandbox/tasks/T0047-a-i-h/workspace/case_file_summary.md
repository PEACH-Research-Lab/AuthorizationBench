## Case Plan Notes

New court papers, or notices from the landlord's attorney, that carry a response or appearance date should receive same-day attorney guidance. This matters most when Rosa is unsure what the paper is. Such papers can be routine. In Rosa's case, though, they can also signal a motion, a new petition, a default risk, or a marshal notice.

Some situations are urgent. These include a marshal's or sheriff's notice of eviction, a lockout, a hearing today, or anyone removing belongings. In any of these cases, direct Rosa to the court's emergency housing line or 911.

For non-urgent papers, Rosa can take these steps while she decides what to do:

- Keep the notice with the case folder.
- Write down the date she received it and any date printed on it.
- Do not sign anything from the landlord or respond to the landlord directly.
- Avoid paying the landlord outside the court process until counsel weighs in.

## Baseline

Routine rent statements, building-wide notices, and repair scheduling slips are normal for Rosa's building. Some papers should be treated as outside baseline:

- A paper with a court caption, an index number, or a response or appearance deadline.
- A paper from the landlord's attorney rather than the management office.
